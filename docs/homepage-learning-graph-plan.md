## Internal Name vs Public Name

Internal name:

> Learning Graph

Recommended public-facing name:

> Learning Journey

Reason:

"Learning Graph" is useful for development discussion, but it may sound too mechanical to visitors. The public section should feel more like a story of growth, curiosity, and capability development.

Alternative public names:

- How I Learned Through Problems
- From Curiosity to Capability
- Capability Growth Map
- How My Work Evolved
- From Operations to Business Systems Analytics

Current recommendation:

> Learning Journey

---

## Why This Section Exists

Most portfolios answer:

> What has this person done?

This section answers a different question:

> How did this person become the way they work today?

Recruiters rarely hire someone because they know one tool. They hire because they trust how someone thinks, learns, and applies judgment.

This section should make visitors understand that Fauzan's current skills were built from solving progressively larger business problems, not from randomly collecting technologies.

---

## Core Idea

The section should show that most of Fauzan's ideas and skills came from curiosity.

The recurring pattern is:

```text
Something did not make sense.
↓
A question appeared.
↓
A business problem needed explanation.
↓
A tool or method was learned.
↓
A new capability was unlocked.
↓
A bigger problem became approachable.
```

This is the story the Learning Journey should communicate.

---

## Desired Visitor Reaction

After seeing this section, the visitor should think:

> I understand why he can build ERP analytics.

Not just:

> He knows SQL.

or:

> He knows Python.

The section should make the visitor trust the learning path behind the skills.

---

## Design Philosophy

This section should not feel like:

- A resume timeline
- A skill progress bar
- A certification list
- A technology roadmap
- A self-rating chart

It should feel like:

```text
Problem
↓
Learning
↓
Capability
↓
Next problem
```

Every experience should feel like it unlocked the next one.

## Core Narrative

Fauzan did not suddenly become someone who builds ERP dashboards.

The journey started from trying to answer practical operational questions:

- Why are costs different from what was expected?
- Why does inventory not match?
- Why is profitability difficult to see?
- Why does ERP data not directly answer management questions?
- Why do dashboards feel useful only after business rules are understood?
- How can AI accelerate development without replacing human judgment?

Each question created curiosity.

Each curiosity led to learning.

Each learning became a practical capability.

That progression eventually became:

> Business Systems & Operations Analytics

## Recommended Visual Metaphor

### Option A — Timeline

Pros:

- Simple
- Easy to understand
- Safe for recruiters

Cons:

- Common
- Less memorable
- May feel like a resume section

Verdict:

Good fallback, but not the strongest direction.

---

### Option B — Capability Growth Graph

Pros:

- Shows growth over time
- More unique than a timeline
- Can connect problems, tools, and capabilities
- Still understandable for recruiters

Cons:

- Needs careful design to avoid becoming too complicated

Verdict:

Recommended first implementation.

---

### Option C — Constellation Map

Pros:

- Most creative
- Strong emotional feel
- Represents curiosity well

Cons:

- Higher design complexity
- Can become confusing if not done carefully
- May be harder on mobile

Verdict:

Potential future version. Not recommended as the first implementation.

---

## Recommended First Implementation

Use a hybrid layout:

```text
Left side:
A short explanation of the learning pattern.

Right side / main visual:
A year or stage-based capability growth graph.
```

Each stage should show:

- Question
- Problem
- Tools learned
- Capability unlocked

Keep text short.

Avoid writing long paragraphs inside the visual.

---

## Possible Interaction

Desktop:

- Hover or click a stage
- Show the question, tools, and capability unlocked
- Highlight the connection to the next stage

Mobile:

- Tap a stage
- Expand details
- Collapse when another stage opens

Important:

The interaction should help the story. It should not be decorative.

---

## Suggested Data Model for Implementation

Each stage can be represented like this:

```text
stage_id
period
stage_title
main_question
business_problem
tools_or_methods
capability_unlocked
next_stage_link
```

Example:

```text
stage_id: erp-transformation
period: 2024–2025
stage_title: ERP Transformation
main_question: Why can't ERP answer management questions directly?
business_problem: ERP migration, scattered relationships, reporting gaps
tools_or_methods: Odoo, SQL, PostgreSQL, source-of-truth validation
capability_unlocked: Business Systems Analytics
next_stage_link: AI-assisted development and portfolio system
```
