# How I Think Through Problems

Status: Approved working philosophy  
Last updated: 6 Jul 2026

This note records the approved homepage direction for the **How I Think Through Problems** section.

## Purpose

This section should explain how Fauzan thinks through real business, data, reporting, and workflow problems. It should not read like a generic dashboard delivery process. It should show that useful solutions begin with discussion, shared understanding, process context, suitable solution design, testing, and practical adoption.

The tone should feel human, reflective, and practical. It should avoid forcing an identity too early. Visitors should understand the thinking pattern through the section itself, together with professional experience, skills, and projects.

## Core philosophy

Fauzan starts from discussion, not tools. He cannot solve a problem if users do not yet see or agree that it is a problem. The first step is to understand whether the issue is a data problem, process problem, perspective problem, thinking problem, unclear-question problem, or a mix of several causes.

Before proposing a solution, Fauzan needs to understand the process end to end: how work and data move from one person, system, document, or decision point to another. Without this, the solution may look advanced but fail in the real environment.

Fauzan prefers suitable solutions, not necessarily the most advanced ones. A solution should fit the users, available resources, business environment, timing, and practical constraints. Similar problems elsewhere can be useful references, but not every solution can be copied directly.

Many problems Fauzan faces are data, interpretation, and repeated-reporting problems. He tends to design systems, dashboards, workflows, or review logic because repetitive reports and unclear interpretation need repeatable structures. He naturally likes automation because repeating the same manual report weekly or monthly is inefficient.

During testing and development, Fauzan may feel uncertain about whether the output is good enough. A strong project core helps reduce overthinking: if the solution answers the original question, improvement can stay focused.

When ready, Fauzan presents the solution, collects feedback, and improves it toward real use. The goal is not only something technically functional, but something users can understand, trust, and use for review, action, or decision-making.

## Homepage section draft

### Section title

How I Think Through Problems

### Section lead

I usually start with discussion, not tools. Before designing a dashboard, workflow, or system, I try to understand whether the issue is really a data problem, a process problem, a perspective problem, or simply an unclear question. A useful solution should fit the users, the environment, the available resources, and the real situation behind the problem.

### Six-step structure

#### 01. Start with discussion

I discuss the situation with users and stakeholders first. Some problems are obvious, but many are hidden behind unclear expectations, different perspectives, missing data, or repeated manual work that people have accepted as normal.

Output: shared problem understanding

#### 02. Understand the process end to end

I trace how the work actually moves: who creates the data, where it changes, where it gets delayed, and how it finally becomes a report, decision, or follow-up action.

Output: process understanding

#### 03. Find a suitable solution

I do not assume the most advanced solution is the best one. The right solution needs to fit the users, the environment, the available resources, and the way people actually work.

Output: practical solution direction

#### 04. Design a system around the problem

Many problems I face are related to data, interpretation, and repeated reporting. I build systems, dashboards, workflows, or review logic to make information easier to understand and act on.

Output: repeatable system design

#### 05. Test and protect the core purpose

After creating a mockup or working version, I test whether it answers the original question. A clear project core helps me improve the solution without overthinking every detail.

Output: tested working version

#### 06. Present, improve, and prepare for real use

When the solution is ready enough, I present it, collect feedback, and refine it toward practical use. The goal is something people can understand, trust, and use for review, action, or decision-making.

Output: usable improvement
