# Portfolio Working Principles

Status: Living design document  
Owner: Fauzan Widianto  
Created: 2026-07-04  
Purpose: Core memory for future portfolio development

---

## Why This Document Exists

This document captures the working patterns that should guide future portfolio development.

The portfolio should not only show finished outputs. It should also reflect how Fauzan thinks, learns, questions, builds, and improves.

These principles are not meant to be displayed directly as a homepage section. They are meant to guide future design decisions, copywriting, project selection, and feature development.

When a new section, animation, project card, or idea is proposed, ask:

> Does this reflect the way Fauzan actually works?

---

## Core Memory

Fauzan is not simply trying to look like a dashboard builder.

The stronger story is:

> Fauzan is curious, system-oriented, and learns by building practical tools that make messy business operations easier to understand.

---

## Working Traits to Reflect

### 1. Curiosity

Evidence:

- Often asks why something works, not only how to implement it.
- Explores AI tools, automation, dashboards, portfolio design, financial markets, and system workflows.
- Frequently turns uncertainty into a learning direction.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio should show continuous learning, not only finished skills.

Possible design expression:

- Learning Graph
- Currently Building
- Thinking Lab
- Notes on what changed or what was learned

---

### 2. Systems Thinking

Evidence:

- Connects sales, procurement, manufacturing, inventory, finance, invoicing, payment, and management review.
- Thinks in workflows, dependencies, source-of-truth logic, and review signals.
- Often tries to understand the relationship between parts before building a solution.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio should show how disconnected information becomes structured visibility.

Possible design expression:

- Process maps
- Translation boards
- Workflow animations
- Review signal logic
- Problem-to-output visuals

---

### 3. Learning by Building

Evidence:

- Learns tools through real projects rather than isolated courses.
- Uses portfolio, Odoo analytics, dashboards, GitHub, Python, SQL, and AI-assisted workflows as practical learning vehicles.
- Iterates through implementation, review, and redesign.

Portfolio implication:

The homepage should show growth through projects and problems, not only a skill list.

Possible design expression:

- Year-by-year learning graph
- Capability growth map
- Tools learned through business problems
- Project evolution notes

---

### 4. Iteration

Evidence:

- Willing to revise a page multiple times when the explanation does not feel right.
- Rejects designs that look polished but do not make sense.
- Improves structure, wording, visuals, and logic step by step.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio should feel actively maintained and thoughtful.

Possible design expression:

- Currently Building section
- Current focus indicator
- Development notes
- Public-safe changelog references

---

### 5. Business-First Judgment

Evidence:

- Starts from business questions, process issues, operational review, and management visibility.
- Treats tools as supporting instruments, not the main identity.
- Prefers dashboards and automation that help people understand what needs follow-up.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio should avoid sounding too developer-heavy or AI-heavy.

Possible design expression:

- Business problem first
- Tool second
- Evidence third
- Clear explanations for non-technical readers

---

### 6. Documentation Discipline

Evidence:

- Builds markdown notes, changelogs, page plans, and project guides.
- Uses documentation to avoid losing direction during development.
- Treats documentation as part of the system, not an afterthought.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio can show selected documentation as proof of structured thinking.

Possible design expression:

- Case study development template
- Page changelogs
- Public-safe documentation links
- Technical architecture explanation

---

### 7. Initiative and Ideas

Evidence:

- Frequently generates ideas for systems, dashboards, workflows, automation, and portfolio improvements.
- Not every idea becomes a finished project, but many reveal useful problem awareness.
- Often identifies friction in daily workflows and imagines practical tools around it.

Portfolio implication:

The portfolio should include a curated way to show ideas without making the site feel unfinished.

Possible design expression:

- Thinking Lab
- Idea cards
- Experiment backlog
- Future systems concepts

Important rule:

Ideas should be curated and framed as exploration, not as completed achievements.

---

## What This Means for Homepage Development

Future homepage development should not only ask:

> What section should we add?

It should ask:

> What part of Fauzan's thinking does this section reveal?

The strongest homepage content will likely come from these three areas:

1. Learning Graph  
   Shows how Fauzan grew through real business problems.

2. Currently Building  
   Shows that the portfolio is alive and actively maintained.

3. Thinking Lab  
   Shows initiative and systems-oriented ideas.

---

## What This Means for Project Pages

Project pages should show more than outputs.

They should show:

- The problem
- The business context
- The messy source reality
- The reasoning process
- The validation logic
- The output
- The lesson learned
- The public-safe limitation

The Odoo ERP Analytics page is the current reference example.

---

## What This Means for CV and LinkedIn

The same working patterns should appear in CV and LinkedIn, but with different levels of detail.

### CV

Keep it concise and evidence-based.

Possible placement:

- Working style
- Selected project descriptions
- Interests
- Profile summary

### LinkedIn

Make it more human and narrative.

Possible themes:

- Learning through building
- Business systems analytics
- Curiosity about AI and financial markets
- Turning messy workflows into reviewable outputs

---

## Design Filter

Before adding something to the portfolio, check whether it reflects at least one of these:

- Curiosity
- Systems thinking
- Learning by building
- Iteration
- Business-first judgment
- Documentation discipline
- Initiative and ideas

If a feature does not reflect any of these, it is likely decoration.

---

## Guiding Sentence

The portfolio should make visitors feel:

> This person is actively learning, thinking in systems, and building practical ways to make business operations easier to understand.
